2012

“Why can't I do this for work?”

Source: diary #3, "Re-Introducing safetyGraphics" (2026-06-16), "A Brief Prehistory". Beats: SAS in grad school (an 80-page master's paper, about 60 pages of it SAS Graph output); Agustin Calatroni introduced R at Rho, pre-tidyverse, lots of lattice; then the NYT interactives. Herman Mitchell said go for it and gave two weeks to learn JavaScript; the first result was "the clunkiest interactive graphic ever", built with Protovis (later D3).

Orange tie-in: Herman also let Jeremy work from Chile for five years, which is where Orange came from.

The Rho Graphics Group

A handful of webpages beats a 200-page PDF

Source: diary #3. The "wall of numbers" in a clinical study report was "100% impenetrable" (the post uses a Harry Potter GIF for the feeling). The Graphics Group at Rho shipped open-source tools in R and JavaScript: ggplot, D3 renderers wrapped as htmlwidgets, hosted in Shiny. The AE Explorer was inspired by Frank Harrell's Hmisc.

Headline paraphrases the post: "a handful of webpages is more useful and usable than a 200-page PDF."

The clinicians did not want a business-intelligence suite; they wanted "a guided clinical monitoring workflow supported by simple tools in their web browser."

ASA–DIA Safety Working Group

safetyGraphics, and the people who built it

  • Clinicians and data scientists, dozens of companies, five years — papers, conferences, the FDA
  • One open-source R package — a Shiny app for exploring a trial's safety data in the browser
  • Jim Buchanan, Susan Mayo, Xiao Ni, Melvin Munsaka, Frank Harrell
  • Agustin Calatroni, Herman Mitchell, Russ Helms and the Rho Graphics Group

A few quiet years

What we did, and what we didn't

We showed

Open-source, interactive graphics work in clinical trials.

We didn't

“Change the industry standard for how people monitor safety.”

Source: diary #3, "A Few Quiet Years". Jeremy stopped active work on safetyGraphics on moving to Gilead in 2021 to work on RBQM. "Those 200-page walls of text still exist in Clinical Study Reports, and safetyGraphics isn't fully GxP-compliant."

The turn into beat 2: "Doing safetyGraphics justice always felt like it needed a team and a budget I no longer have — but with AI agents, I'm honestly not sure that's true anymore. Figuring out whether it is, is what this whole keynote experiment is about." Then: the OpenRBQM team "is already dragging clinical monitoring toward something more modern."
