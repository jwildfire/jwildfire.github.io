Costs

$11,750 at API prices. I paid $200 a month.

The record has holes: June is not counted, and most of September is missing.

DRAFT, 2026-10-05. Every word on this slide is draft wording for Jeremy to replace; the numbers are sourced (below, and in beat4-spending-sources.md). Counted on the evening of 5 October Eastern, which is 6 October UTC; the data's days are UTC days.

Where the numbers come from: the hub's analytics page, jwildfire.github.io/obot.roadmap/analytics/, and its data file usage/usage.json. The page was brought forward from 19 August to 6 October on 2026-10-05 (obot.roadmap pull request 370). Until that pull request is merged the live page still shows the August numbers and the figures here are the branch's.

What "at API prices" means: tokens recorded in Claude Code's session logs, multiplied by Anthropic's list prices. It is what the same usage would bill through the API. It is not an invoice and it is not what was paid.

Talking points
1. The bars, one per obot version, at API list prices.
• v3, July: $3,329.60. 3.43 billion tokens, 17,283 requests, 14 days with usage.
• v4, 1 August to 10 September: at least $6,020.26. The solid part, $3,830.50, is 1 to 19 August and was recorded in full. The hatched part, $2,189.76, is 20 August to 10 September and is a floor.
• v5, 11 to 30 September: at least $451.72, on three days (11, 12 and 18 September), including the one cloud session that published its usage ($8.85). All of it is a floor.
• v5.5, October: $1,954.96 for 1 to 6 October, 4.82 billion tokens, all on Opus 5.5. Still running.
• Together: $11,756.53 on record, 15.7 billion tokens, 61,351 requests, 54 days with any usage.
2. Why the hatched bars are floors. The nightly job that was meant to read the logs never ran (macOS would not let it open a script in the Documents folder), and Claude Code deletes a session log it has not touched for 30 days. When the logs were finally read on 5 October, what was left of 20 August to 18 September was mostly the two coordinating sessions. In the last week recorded in full, 14 to 18 August, those two were 16% of the spend ($570 of $3,625). From 10 September the work also moved to cloud sessions, and one of them published its usage. Nothing at all is recorded for 19 to 30 September; nothing was merged on GitHub in those days either (research digest 8g).
3. What I paid. Diary #6: “All of the work was done on a $200/month Claude Max plan.” Diary #8: “The $7k is API-equivalent spend, not out of pocket costs.” Diary #9's footnote adds the catch: “When the weekly allowance ran out in August, the work paused until it reset.” So the plan's real price was sometimes waiting.
4. The price per token fell by more than half. Everything in July: $0.97 per million tokens. Everything in October: $0.41. (9 July to 19 August together: $0.92.) Two reasons, both list prices: Opus 5.5 is $4 in and $20 out per million tokens against $5 and $25 for Opus 5 and $10 and $50 for Fable 5; and it reads its cache at $0.20 per million against $0.50 and $1.00. About 97% of all the tokens here are cache reads.
5. Two long weekends. July, Friday 10 to Sunday 12: “just over 1 billion tokens”, $1,273 (diary #6). October, Friday 2 to Sunday 4: one orchestration session, 3.94 billion tokens, $1,639.07, 9,822 requests. Nearly four times the tokens for 1.3 times the price.
6. For the GxP question (draft): this is what it costs when one person runs it on a consumer plan. A regulated team would pay API or enterprise prices, so the bars are closer to their bill than the $200 is. Jeremy to say it his way.
