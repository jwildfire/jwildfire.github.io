The whole roadmap is one tree of GitHub issues

obot.roadmap · as of 7 October 2026 · 44 retired requirements and 25 with no objective not shown

DRAFT, 2026-10-05. The headline and kicker are draft wording for Jeremy to replace. The picture is generated, not drawn by hand; the numbers below come from the same run.

What it shows
Every open line of work on the hub as nested boxes. A grey block is an objective. Inside it, each framed box is a requirement in its status colour, and each small cell inside that is one task. Area is the number of tasks, with a requirement that has no tasks yet counted as one so it still shows. The bar beside an objective's name is the share of its requirements in each status.
Colours are the tracker's: green is released (and a task that is done), dark plum is in review, mid plum is in session, light plum is ready, the palest tint is backlog (and a task still open).

The point (draft)
1. This is the answer to "how do we do this in GxP?" at the level of process: nothing is worked on that is not on this picture. An objective is mine. Each requirement under it has a definition of done written before any code. Each task is one pull request.
2. I do not read the code as it is written. I set the objectives, sign off the tree, answer the questions that block it, and review the releases. "I review releases and answer questions."
3. The green is what shipped; the pale blocks are what I have asked for and nobody has started. Both are on the record, with dates, in public.

How it was made
keynote/_notes/roadmap-tree/: fetch.mjs reads GitHub through the hub tracker's own collector and saves a trimmed model; build.mjs lays out the treemap and writes the SVG and the caption into this slide. Refresh it before the talk with the two commands in that folder's README; the date in the caption changes with it.

Read this before showing it
- Retired requirements are left out, and so are the requirements filed under no objective. The caption says how many. Most of the retired ones belong to the autonomy objective, the experiment that was shut down in September.
- Six pale backlog boxes are full of green cells: the tasks are done but the label on the requirement was never moved. It is label drift on the hub, not unfinished work.
- Four small objectives (the keynote deck, Static parity, the desktop tool, Program operations) are drawn a little larger than their task count, in the column on the right, so that their names fit at a readable size. Every other block is to scale. roadmap-tree/counts.md lists them after each run.
- No requirement is named on this slide: none of their titles fits in its box at 18px, and a cut-off title reads worse than none. The next slide names them.
- The one hatched cell is a task that was closed as not planned.

What it shows
One block of the previous slide, opened up: the Biomarker charts objective on the left, a line to each of its requirements, and after each requirement one bead per task.
