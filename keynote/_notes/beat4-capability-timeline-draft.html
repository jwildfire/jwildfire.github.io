How fast it is moving

Eleven months, and I could not keep up

We cannot keep up, so the gap between capability and adoption keeps widening.

DRAFT, 2026-10-05. Every word on this slide is draft wording for Jeremy to replace; the dates are sourced (list at the end, and in beat4-capability-timeline-sources.md).

Talking points
1. The chart starts on 24 November 2025, the day Opus 4.5 came out. Simon Willison calls November 2025 the inflection point: Opus 4.5 and GPT-5.1, paired with Claude Code and Codex, went from often making mistakes to “reliable enough to use on a day-to-day basis”. This talk is 331 days later. That is eleven months, not quite a year.
2. Above the line, eight releases picked for the story, not all of them. OpenClaw got its name by the end of January (its first commit was 24 November 2025, the same day as Opus 4.5). Mythos was announced on 7 April and held back from the public. Fable 5 came out on 9 June, was suspended on 12 June and came back on 1 July. GPT-5.6 followed on 9 July, Opus 5 on 24 July, Fable 5.1 on 1 September, and Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Sol and Luna on the same day, 22 September (GPT-6 Astra was 3 September).
3. Below the line, my versions: v1 to v5, then v5.5, all inside the last five months. The coloured markers are what each one ran on. The wait got shorter each time: OpenClaw was named in January and v2 started on 11 May; Fable 5 came back on 1 July and v3's billion-token weekend was 10–12 July; Opus 5 came out on 24 July and v4 ran through August to 10 September; Fable 5.1 came out on 1 September and v5 (one requirement at a time, 10 to 30 September) ran on it and on Opus 5; Opus 5.5 came out on 22 September and v5.5 started on it on 1 October.
4. What I did not try (the hollow markers). MCP, the Model Context Protocol: an open standard for connecting agents to tools and data, out since 25 November 2024, so older than everything on this chart. Muse: Meta's personal agent, 8 September. Jev: TypeSafe's “System One” model, which returns typed decisions with probabilities instead of text, 15 September. Dots: OpenAI's always-on personal agents, each with its own cloud computer, 29 September. Those last three arrived in the six weeks before this talk.
5. Open-weight models were out of scope, and that matters here. All six versions ran on closed, hosted models. Willison names the improvement in open-weight models, including ones that run on a laptop, as a key trend of 2026. His two examples: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, a 21GB file he ran on his laptop on 16 April, and Qwen 3.8 27B, Apache 2 licensed, a 17GB download released on 14 August, the first local model he found almost competitive with the frontier (on his pelican test). For an industry with data that cannot leave the building, that is the lane to watch, and I did not test it.
6. Takeaway: one person with six tries could not keep up. The standards we work under change slowly by design (diary #9: FDA's guidance on AI in drug development has been a draft since January 2025). So the gap between what agents can do and what we have adopted is widening.
