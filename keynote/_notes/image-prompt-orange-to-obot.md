# Image brief: Orange → obot hexes

Drafted 2026-10-05 for an image model. Attach the reference images listed at the end.

---

I need a set of hexagon sticker illustrations for a conference keynote. Together they show a real cat, Orange, turning into a robot cat called obot, one step at a time. Please read the whole brief before drawing anything, then start with step 1 only.

## Context

- The talk: "Building open-source clinical trial tools with agentic AI", the R/Pharma 2026 keynote, by Jeremy Wildfire. Talk page: https://jwildfire.github.io/keynote/
- The deck (HTML source): https://github.com/jwildfire/jwildfire.github.io/blob/claude/eloquent-einstein-npcq1y/keynote/slides.html
  - The copy at that link is older than the slides this brief is about. The attached screenshots show the current slides; treat them as the source of truth.
- Orange was Jeremy's cat: a Chilean street cat, very silly, who sat with him while he worked. He opens the talk and comes back on every section-break slide. The AI agent in the talk is named after him ("obot" = Orange + bot).
- Orange is remembered fondly. The tone is warm and a bit silly, never creepy, sad or menacing.
- The audience is R programmers in pharma. They know R package hex stickers well, so these should read as real hex stickers.

## Where the art goes

Slide 28 of 55, "A summer down the AI rabbit hole, with obot" (screenshot attached). It is a row of hexes joined by arrows: Orange, then each version of obot. It is both the "Orange becomes a robot" picture and the map of the section that follows. The robot cats on it now are placeholders I want to replace.

The same hexes are reused at three sizes, so each one has to read at all three:

- About 56 px wide, as the bullet next to a slide's kicker line
- About 150 px wide, in the row on slide 28
- About 380 px wide, alone on the right of each version's title slide (slide 31 attached as an example)

The deck adds the arrows, names and captions in HTML. Do not draw the slide, the arrows or any captions; I only need the hexes.

## The character

- Orange: a ginger tabby. Warm orange coat with darker tabby stripes and the tabby "M" on the forehead, amber-green eyes, pink nose, long white whiskers, large upright ears, a slightly worried, earnest look. Two photos attached; match him, not a generic cartoon cat.
- obot: Orange, rebuilt as a small friendly robot. Keep what makes him Orange through every version: the orange colouring, the forehead stripes, the ear shape, the whiskers, the pink nose. Robot parts are added on top: an antenna, panel seams, rivets, glowing cyan eyes.
- The transformation is the point. Each version is a little more robot and a little more capable than the last, so the row reads left to right as one cat changing:
  - Orange: all cat
  - v1: mostly cat, the first robot parts
  - v3: about half and half
  - v5.5: fully robot, sleek and confident, still unmistakably Orange
- Head and shoulders only, facing the viewer, centred, the same pose and scale in every hex.

## Style

- Flat vector sticker art: solid fills, one bold dark outline in `#1f2328`, no gradients, no photographic texture, no 3D render, no drop shadows.
- A small palette: the hex's background colour, Orange's coat (`#c78a3b` and a lighter and a darker orange), off-white `#f3f4f1`, ink `#1f2328`, pink `#d87972` for the nose, cyan `#7fe3df` for robot eyes.
- Friendly proportions: big head, big eyes. Closer to a well-made R package hex than to a mascot or an emoji.
- Typography in the deck is Instrument Serif, Instrument Sans and IBM Plex Mono, on an off-white `#fafaf8` slide. The art should sit comfortably beside that: clean, a little editorial, not glossy.

## Format

- A regular hexagon with a point at the top and bottom (the standard R hex sticker shape), width to height 1 : 1.1547.
- 1040 × 1200 px PNG per hex, transparent outside the hexagon, the artwork filling the hexagon edge to edge.
- One file per hex, named `orange.png`, `obot-v1.png`, `obot-v2.png`, `obot-v2-5.png`, `obot-v3.png`, `obot-v4.png`, `obot-v5.png`, `obot-v5-5.png`.
- Text: only the version label ("v1", "v2", "v2.5" … "v5.5") in small white monospace at the bottom point of the hex, and nothing on the Orange hex. If the label does not come out clean, leave it off and I will add it in the deck.

## Please avoid

- Any real logos or brand marks: no GitHub, Telegram, Anthropic, Claude or OpenAI marks, and no R logo.
- Any other words, letters or numbers in the art.
- Anything menacing or uncanny: no red eyes, exposed wiring, skulls, cyborg body horror or "evil AI" imagery.
- Halos, angel wings or other memorial imagery.
- Copying the attached forcats sticker or any existing hex. It is there only to show the convention.

## How I would like to work

1. First, a character sheet on a plain off-white background: Orange and the fully robot v5.5 side by side, plus one in-between stage. No hexes yet. I will react to the likeness and the style before anything else is drawn.
2. Then the eight hexes, one at a time in the order of the table, each drawn from the approved character sheet so the set stays consistent.
3. Last, and only if the set works: one wide strip, about 3 : 1, of Orange turning into obot in five or six stages with no hexes and no text, as an alternative picture for slide 28.
